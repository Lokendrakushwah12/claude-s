# Can assistant-written text score human on Pangram? (evidence, 2026-10-09)

Web sweep of benchmarks, papers and vendor tests; every claim re-checked against its source by a second agent. Public research only, no workspace text.

Pangram 4 (v4) shipped on 29 Jul 2026. API calls that named no model stayed on Pangram 3 until its scheduled retirement on 30 Sep 2026 (https://www.pangram.com/blog/introducing-pangram-4), so it is unknown which model scored the user's tests. Most published bypasses were tested before v4.

Prompt-level humanizing, which is what the human-voice skill does, fails on v4. Telling an Opus 5 agent to avoid AI tells raised its fraction_AI (the share of text scored as AI) from 0.774 to 0.820 on long answers (Duke/IISc draft, Sep 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31876). Pangram missed 0.43% of 10,223 texts rewritten with blader/humanizer, a skill built on the same Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" page (vendor-run, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27183; https://github.com/blader/humanizer). Nobody has tested prompt rewrites of short chat messages. Style imitation did better on older versions. Given five of an author's real passages, frontier models got 10.1% of ~500-word texts past v3.3.2, and 6.1% were scored outright Human (Epoch, Jul 2026, https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives). When Pangram re-ran that set, v4 had 17 misses against 30 for v3.3.2.

Pangram mechanics
- Minimum length: The dashboard needs 50 words ("Below that, there isn't enough signal to analyze reliably", https://www.pangram.com/knowledge-hub/minimum-and-maximum-input-sizes); before v3.2 the minimum was 75 (https://www.pangram.com/blog/introducing-pangram-3-2). v4 "excludes short, conversational replies from Pangram's scope" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27183). Even so, the v4 report scored under-50-word texts and caught 99.70% of plain AI and 73.32% of humanizer output at 1% FPR. The API documents no minimum. A failed scan returns HTTP 200, STAGE_FAILED and fraction_ai 0.0, which is not a human verdict (https://docs.pangram.com/api-reference/ai-detection.md).
- Mixed authorship: Each token is labeled Human, AI-Assisted or AI-Generated. A document is Human only if at least 90% of its characters are human, AI if at least 80% are AI-generated, and Mixed otherwise (https://www.pangram.com/research/model-card/pangram-4). Pangram reports that 99.45% of lightly AI-polished human essays stay Human (n=11,363). In independent testing on v3.2, light AI "refine" edits of human abstracts were flagged 64.4–80.1% of the time (Notre Dame). On v3.3, 239 human words plus 71 ChatGPT words scored 100% AI (https://freddiedeboer.substack.com/p/i-wouldnt-say-pangram-is-broken-but).
- Hinglish: No public test of romanized or code-mixed text exists for any version. The v4 report gives Hindi (FPR 0.0000%, FNR 1.37%) and Urdu (FNR 5.32%) without naming the script. IndicDetect excludes code-mixed and transliterated text and does not test Pangram (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29919).
- Dictation: No published test covers dictated or Wispr Flow text on Pangram. Wispr Flow removes fillers, adds punctuation and restyles text per app (https://wisprflow.ai/). Pangram counts filler and punctuation fixes as light assistance, but tone or style changes as moderate (https://www.pangram.com/knowledge-hub/what-is-ai-assisted). In an informal test, 52- and 65-word meeting-transcript excerpts scored 100% AI (https://karozieminski.substack.com/p/substack-pangram-ai-detector-experiment-results).
- False positives: v4 reports 0.0041% (95% CI 0.0032–0.0050%) on English web text, and one false positive in 24,586 non-native texts. False positives cluster on short or excerpted text:
  - A 50-word excerpt reported by The Atlantic, per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangram_(AI_detector).
  - A ~300-word section of a human essay that scored 100% AI on its own (deBoer, v3.3).
  - Under-50-word stubs, flagged at up to 2.5% (API v2025-05, https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/BFI_WP_2025-116.pdf).
- Unicode handling: v4 "removes adversarial Unicode characters and normalizes mixed-script homoglyph attacks before tokenization" (model card). Its humanizer head is built to flag deliberate homoglyphs, typo injection and casing changes (is_humanized, default threshold 0.91), but not incidental encoding errors (v4 report). It is undocumented whether the stripped characters themselves raise is_humanized.

On Pangram 4, no independently verified method gets assistant-written text to score human without corrupting it. Prompting, a skill built on Wikipedia's list of AI tells, paraphrase, a 15-query detector loop and homoglyphs all fail on v4. Typo injection failed on an earlier model and is something v4's humanizer head is built to flag. Style imitation passes only occasionally. The one independent success is HALO, an agent that builds answers mostly from samples of base (non-chat) models. It cut fraction_AI from 0.774 to 0.245, with quality falling from 0.871 to 0.831. But it costs 9–27x more per response in API fees, plus two A6000 GPUs. It was tested only on long answers, and 15–19% of its outputs were still labeled AI, with 17–19% Mixed (Duke/IISc). Two commercial humanizers pass v4 on long-form text by vendor counts: Phrasly 26/30 in a rival's benchmark and 72/72 in its own, and StealthGPT 22/30 to 89/100. No independent party has checked these, and none of the tests used short messages. By Phrasly's own count, 51% of its outputs contain an unsupported factual change (94% for StealthGPT Super), so they pass by changing what the text says. Before v4 the answer was yes. A detector-guided prompt loop cut recall to 24.1%, with the content judged intact. A model fine-tuned on one author's books was flagged only 3% of the time at a 0.9 threshold (2025, n=30, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939). Neither has been re-tested on v4. Pangram 4 has two real weak spots, but they come from the kind of text, not from a technique. First, it puts short conversational replies outside its scope; the 52-word note sits just above that floor, where human text also gets misflagged. Second, terse notes built from more context than they contain went undetected: this was the red team's one bypass, and unattacked FACTS Grounding answers scored 0.130 fraction_AI. For an ordinary short message, the only documented way to get a Human verdict with the content intact is the one already observed. The user types it, or rewrites it until Pangram reads at least 90% of the characters as human.
